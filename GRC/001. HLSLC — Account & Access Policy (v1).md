## E001 -HLSLC — Account & Access Policy (v1)

**Document ID:** HLSLC-GRC-POL-001
**Version:** 1.0
**Classification:** Internal
**Owner:** GRC
**Consumer:** IT Ops (this document is the specification the AD build implements against)
**Review cycle:** Annual, or upon material change to scope

---

## 1. Scenario

HomeLab Simulated Logistic Company (HLSLC) is a small, newly-modeled freight/logistics company about to have its first Active Directory environment built. GRC is establishing the baseline access standard before IT Ops provisions a single account.

## 2. Business problem

Without a documented standard preceding implementation, account creation defaults to ad hoc judgment calls — inconsistent naming, inconsistent privilege levels, undefined approval authority, and no defined offboarding trigger. Individually minor, these gaps compound into the organization being unable to answer the standard audit question: *who has access to what, on what basis, and who approved it.*

## 3. Access risk

This policy's control objectives map to four risk categories:

| Risk category | Description |
|---|---|
| Excessive entitlement | Access provisioned beyond the minimum required for the assigned role. |
| Undocumented authorization | Access granted without a recorded requester, approver, and business justification. |
| Persistent unnecessary access | Access that outlives the need for it due to absent review or offboarding triggers. |
| Inadequate segregation of duties | A single identity able to both execute and approve the same class of transaction. |

## 4. What we built

*This entry reflects the **On-Premises** stage of HLSLC's identity maturity roadmap (On-Premises → Hybrid → Cloud-Native) — a self-managed Active Directory environment with no cloud identity dependency. Hybrid (Entra Connect-synced) and Cloud-Native (Entra ID + Sentinel) stages are planned as later, separate entries.*

### 4.1 Purpose

To define mandatory controls governing identity lifecycle management — account creation, authentication, authorization, review, and termination — for all HLSLC systems.

### 4.2 Scope

Applies to all user and administrative accounts on HLSLC-managed systems (Active Directory, department applications, and any system requiring individual authentication). Excludes non-human/service accounts, which are governed under a separate control (deferred to v2).

### 4.3 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Least privilege | Access limited to the minimum permissions required to perform an assigned function. |
| Segregation of duties (SoD) | No single identity holds both execution and approval authority over the same class of transaction. |
| Provisioning | The technical act of granting approved access. |
| Deprovisioning | The technical act of revoking access no longer required. |
| Standard account | An identity used for routine, non-privileged work. |
| Privileged account | An identity authorized to perform administrative actions, held separately from any standard account for the same individual. |
| Data owner | The role accountable for authorizing access to a defined data set (e.g., Accounting Manager for financial records). |

### 4.4 Policy statements

**4.4.1 Identity and naming standards**
Usernames shall follow the format `{first initial}{last name}`, lowercase. Privileged accounts shall be issued as a distinct identity from the standard account, suffixed `-adm`, and shall not be used for routine work.

**4.4.2 Authentication requirements**
Memorized secrets shall be a minimum of 12 characters. Composition rules (mandatory mixed case/digit/symbol) shall not be enforced as the primary control; length is the primary control, consistent with NIST SP 800-63B §5.1.1 guidance. Mandatory periodic rotation shall not be enforced absent evidence of compromise. Accounts shall lock after 5 failed authentication attempts within a 15-minute window. Multi-factor authentication shall be required for all privileged (`-adm`) accounts; extension to standard accounts is deferred to v2 pending MFA infrastructure.

**4.4.3 Least privilege and provisioning**
Access requests shall record requester identity, target role, requested systems, and business justification. Provisioning shall grant only the security group(s) corresponding to the approved request; no additional group membership shall be bundled without a separate, independently justified request.

**4.4.4 Approval authority and segregation of duties**
Standard department access requires approval from the requesting employee's manager. Access to the Finance-Data-Access group additionally requires approval from the Accounting Manager as data owner. No individual may approve their own access request.

**4.4.5 Access review**
Standing access shall be subject to periodic recertification to confirm continued business need. Review findings that identify unjustified access shall trigger deprovisioning under §4.4.6.

**4.4.6 Deprovisioning**
Upon role change or departure, the account shall be disabled on the effective date and removed from all security groups pending final deletion. Full deletion shall occur after a 30-day retention window, except for fixed-term/seasonal personnel, whose accounts may be retained in a disabled state across terms at manager discretion. Accounts holding no data and no handoff dependency (e.g., internal test/validation accounts) may be deleted immediately without a retention window.

### 4.5 Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Requesting manager | Submits and approves standard access requests for direct reports. |
| Data owner | Approves access to data sets under their ownership (e.g., Finance-Data-Access). |
| IT Ops | Provisions and deprovisions accounts per approved requests; never grants access outside an approved request. |
| GRC | Owns this policy, defines review cadence, and audits adherence. |

### 4.7 Enforcement and exceptions

Access provisioned in violation of this policy shall be revoked upon discovery. Exceptions require documented GRC approval, a defined expiration, and are subject to review at the next recertification cycle.

## 5. Evidence

None at this stage — this document is the standard, not an implementation. Evidence (OU structure, group membership exports, screenshots) is produced in the IT Ops entry that implements it.

## 6. Recommendation

Pass this document to IT Ops as the build specification: OUs per department, security groups per §4.4.1–4.4.4, and the roster (v1) as the provisioning list. Any control proving impractical to implement as written should be flagged as feedback, not silently deviated from — that feedback is the input to v2.
